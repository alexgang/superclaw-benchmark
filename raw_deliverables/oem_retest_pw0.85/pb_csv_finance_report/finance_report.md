## 1. Executive Summary

Apple delivered a strong 2014, with the stock advancing on the back of a successful iPhone 6 cycle, the June 7-for-1 stock split, and continued growth in services revenue. The adjusted closing price rose from **$77.45 on 2014-01-02** to **$110.03 on 2014-12-12** — the final observation in the dataset — producing a total return of **+42.07%** over the ~11.5-month window.

Despite a soft start (the stock fell ~10.9% in January on disappointing iPhone holiday-quarter results and emerging-market weakness), Apple reversed course and trended higher for most of the year. The stock printed its 2014 low of **$69.00 on 2014-01-31**, then rallied almost uninterrupted into late November, hitting an intra-year high of **$118.80 on 2014-11-28** before pulling back into year-end.

**Interpretation:**

- **January selloff** dominated the year's downside — Apple gave back roughly 12% in four weeks after earnings.
- **Spring rally (mid-April through early June)** was the year's strongest leg, ~+16% over three weeks following the strong fiscal-Q2 print and ahead of the June 9 stock-split effective date. The stock rallied into the split (announcement April 23; effective June 9).
- **Mid-summer grind higher** (late June through late July) carried the stock from the low-$90s into the high-$90s in advance of the September iPhone event.
- **September dip** was a textbook pre-event de-risking — the stock bottomed in mid-September and then rallied sharply after the September 19 launch.
- **October–November melt-up** took the stock from ~$97 to ~$118 (~+22%) as fiscal-Q4 results validated the iPhone 6 cycle and Q1 FY15 guidance pointed to further upside.
- **December pullback** erased some of those gains on profit-taking.

### 5.3 Observable Patterns

- **Strong directional bias to the upside:** 145 of 239 trading days were up (60.7%), 94 down (39.3%), and only 1 flat.
- **Earnings-driven gap days dominate the tails of the return distribution:** Four of the six largest absolute moves (Jan 28, Apr 24, Oct 21, Dec 2) clustered within ±1 trading day of a quarterly earnings release.
- **Range-bound trading in late Feb–early Apr:** Between the post-January rebound and the April rally, the stock consolidated in the $73–$77 zone for ~6 weeks — a roughly 5% range that resolved to the upside.
- **Pre-event sell-the-news behavior:** Visible ahead of both the September product event (early September dip) and the November peak (early December pullback).

## 7. Conclusion

**Key Takeaways**

1. **Strong absolute performance:** Apple returned **+42.07%** over the 239-trading-day window covered by the dataset (Jan 2 – Dec 12, 2014), with an annualized return of roughly **+44.8%** — multiples of the S&P 500's ~11% total return in calendar 2014.

2. **Concentrated risk in January:** The year's only material drawdown (-10.90%) occurred in the four weeks following fiscal-Q1 2014 earnings. Once the stock recovered that drawdown by April 24, it never came close to retesting it; the subsequent trend was higher for most of the year.

3. **Asymmetric return distribution:** 60.7% up days, supported by a 9-day winning streak in August and a 13-week grind higher into the November high. The largest single down day (-7.51%) was a one-off earnings reaction that quickly reversed.

4. **Event-driven volatility, not regime volatility:** The largest moves clustered around earnings and the September 9 product event. Intra-quarter volatility (Q3 at 20.7% annualized) was actually lower than Q1 or Q4, suggesting the calmest stretch of the year was the build-up to the iPhone 6 launch.

5. **Excellent risk-adjusted return:** A Sharpe-like ratio of ~1.9 is exceptional for a single-name mega-cap and indicates Apple delivered strong return per unit of risk in 2014.

**Overall Assessment**

2014 was a vintage year for AAPL. After a weak January, the stock re-rated higher on (i) the fiscal-Q2 beat and buyback, (ii) the 7-for-1 split announcement, (iii) the highly successful iPhone 6 / 6 Plus launch in September, and (iv) fiscal-Q4 results confirming record September-quarter unit sales. The combination of a clearly positive fundamental catalyst (the iPhone 6 super-cycle), a structural return-of-capital event (split + expanded buyback), and a benign macro backdrop produced both strong absolute returns and a favorable risk-adjusted profile. The principal risk for an investor entering at the December close was mean-reversion: after a ~42% run in roughly a calendar year, the stock was pricing in a continuation of the iPhone 6 cycle, leaving little margin for a disappointing March- or June-quarter print.
